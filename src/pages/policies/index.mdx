# Policies and support

## Attendance

The crit is the course. There's no lecture recording that substitutes for
being in the room when your work is up, and none that substitutes for having
given a note to someone else's. Miss more than two crits without arranging
it first with Marisol and your final portfolio mark is capped at a pass,
regardless of what's in it --- a portfolio of notes you gave in a room you
weren't in isn't credible.

## Late work

Assignment 1 and the Peer Review Exchange are due at noon on their listed
date. A day late costs 10% of that assessment's mark; after five days it
isn't accepted, because a note delivered a week after the work it's about is
a different, less useful thing than a note delivered on time. The Final
Project follows the university's standard late-submission rule instead, since
by week 12 "a week late" no longer changes what the feedback is for.

Extensions granted before the deadline are not penalties; ask early.

## Academic integrity

Write your own notes. You may discuss a piece of work with a classmate before
you write about it --- crits are collaborative by design --- but the note
your name is on has to be the judgement you'd defend out loud. If you use a
tool to help you draft, say so in the note itself: a note that hides where it
came from fails this course's own test for a note, because nobody can
falsify a claim about work they don't know was made by a person.

## What a note is not allowed to be

This is a course about how to be honest without being cruel, and the two get
confused often enough that it's worth a rule. A note about the person rather
than the work --- their effort, their intelligence, their taste as a
character trait rather than a choice they made on this piece --- isn't
marked as a strong note. It's marked as a failed one, on the same criteria as
a vague one: it isn't about anything you could point to, so it can't be
falsified, so it isn't a note.

## Getting help

Ask in the crit first. Email Marisol for anything about the course as a
whole; email Idris for anything technical the room didn't have time for. See
[People](/people/) for both.
