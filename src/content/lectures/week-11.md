A method that works every single week for ten weeks running is suspicious
in a specific way: it means nobody's tested it against a domain where it's
actually uncomfortable to apply. The honest version of this course's claim
isn't "specific and falsifiable and about the work travels everywhere
cleanly" --- it's "here's exactly where it got harder, and why."

It got harder in prose, because the sentence and the person who wrote it are
harder to pull apart than a line of code and its author. It got easier, in a
way worth being suspicious of, in code review, where the culture already
supplies the vocabulary for staying on the artefact. And it got strangest
turned on the brief itself in week 9, where the target of the note had
power over the person giving it, which nothing before that week had tested.

A portfolio that only shows the domains where the method worked easily
isn't evidence the method works. A portfolio that includes the domain you
were worst at, with an honest account of why, is.

## Outline

- where the method held cleanly, and where it strained
- why "it worked every week" would be evidence against the course, not for it
- what the Final Project portfolio has to include to be credible rather than
  curated
