Everything up to this week has been a note on a piece of work someone chose
to make. A brief is different: it's the instruction that shapes what work
gets made at all, and it's usually written by whoever is going to mark the
result. That asymmetry is exactly why this week matters --- if the method
only works on things you have no stake in criticising, it isn't a method,
it's a permission structure.

The test doesn't change: a note on a brief still has to name a specific
clause, and say what would prove the complaint wrong. "The brief is
unclear" fails the same way "the pacing drags" failed in week 3 --- it names
nothing a person could go and fix. "Clause two says 'clear and concise' but
gives no way to tell the two apart" survives the same argument a note on a
paragraph would have to survive.

This is also, deliberately, the week this course points the method at
itself. A brief that can't survive its own test doesn't get to keep being
exempt from it just because it's the one handing out the marks.
