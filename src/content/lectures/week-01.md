A note is a claim about a piece of work, specific enough to point at and
falsifiable enough to argue with. Most of what gets called feedback is
neither: a reaction dressed as an assessment, unfalsifiable because it never
said what would change its mind, and usually aimed at the person who made
the thing rather than the thing itself.

That's a strong claim to make about something as old as criticism, so this
course doesn't just assert it once and move on --- it tests the same claim
against a drawing, a pull request, a paragraph, a blind proposal, a
restaurant, and the brief you were handed to do all of that. If the method
only survives in the domain it was invented for, it isn't a method. It's a
habit that happens to work on one kind of work.

## Outline

- what makes a note different from a reaction: specific, falsifiable, about
  the work
- why "the work, not the person" is harder than it sounds, and gets harder
  in prose than in code
- the plan for the semester: one new domain most weeks, the same three-part
  test applied to each
